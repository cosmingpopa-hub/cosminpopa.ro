Many students treat anatomy as a long list of terms. The issue is usually not effort, but strategy. If you study only for the exam, you forget quickly. If you study for clinical context, the knowledge stays.

Step one is to organize by regions and clear goals: what you must know, what you must identify on imaging, and what you must explain clinically.

Step two is active recall: draw simplified maps, explain structures out loud, and self-test daily for 15 to 20 minutes.

Step three is imaging integration. Match atlas structures with CT, MRI, or CBCT whenever possible. That is when anatomy moves from page to practice.

Anatomy is not a memory contest; it is the foundation of clinical thinking. With method, you do more than pass exams, you build durable professional competence.
